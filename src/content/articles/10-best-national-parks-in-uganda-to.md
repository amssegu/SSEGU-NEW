<p> </p><h1 data-end="53" data-section-id="1f9gmnf" data-start="0" dir="auto">10 Best National Parks in Uganda to Visit Before You Die<span aria-hidden="true"></span></h1><div><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjSrqtKEARvzMNP8vz7SgFQFswIs_j8E75hsKPY5k16uPAo6PPnZRtl0p5BHeKjAAwlYkYscX87_JxCP5bWZ5JsuoJmtFQD0CNI5lGyveWV3dkVkiYShO5iAETSV_PuW9kQldcAmafTBUKHJMHSx-x4hFDDeetTnu1daZdyM_hcpPY7aAeLNfevdvIo8Ro/s1032/top%2010%20national%20parks%20uganda.webp"></a></div><br><div><br></div><p data-end="364" data-start="55" dir="auto">Uganda is unusually good for travellers who want more than a conventional safari. Within the country’s national-park system, you can watch elephants and lions on the savannah, follow chimpanzees through tropical forest, trek towards mountain gorillas, hike volcanic slopes and spend days in remote wilderness.</p><p data-end="406" data-start="366" dir="auto">The tricky part is choosing where to go.</p><p data-end="629" data-start="408" dir="auto">Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) currently manages <strong data-end="479" data-start="458">10 national parks</strong>, ranging from Murchison Falls in the north-west to Mount Elgon in the east and Kidepo Valley in the north-east.<span data-content-reference-end="624" data-content-reference-start="592"><span data-state="closed"></span></span></p><p data-end="840" data-start="631" dir="auto">There is no objectively “best” park. The right choice depends on whether you want a classic game drive, primates, gorillas, hiking, birding, scenery, a short trip from Kampala or a proper wilderness adventure.</p><p data-end="905" data-start="842" dir="auto">Here are the 10 parks worth putting on your Uganda travel list.</p><h2 data-end="972" data-section-id="1serywu" data-start="907" dir="auto">1. Murchison Falls National Park — for a classic Uganda safari</h2>Murchison Falls is one of Uganda's defining safari destinations.<p data-end="1261" data-start="1082" dir="auto">The park combines broad savannah landscapes with the Victoria Nile and the dramatic Murchison Falls, where the river is forced through a narrow gorge before continuing downstream.</p><p data-end="1481" data-start="1263" dir="auto">For many visitors, the attraction is the combination of <strong data-end="1355" data-start="1319">game drives and a Nile boat trip</strong> rather than a single activity. The landscape also gives the trip a distinctly different character from Uganda's forest parks.</p><p data-end="1698" data-start="1483" dir="auto">Murchison Falls is large, so accommodation and activity locations matter when planning your itinerary. Staying on one side of the park can make some activities considerably less convenient than they appear on a map.</p><p data-end="1796" data-start="1700" dir="auto"><strong data-end="1713" data-start="1700">Best for:</strong> first-time safari travellers, wildlife viewing, river scenery and multi-day trips.</p><p data-end="1887" data-start="1798" dir="auto"><strong data-end="1811" data-start="1798">Plan for:</strong> at least 2–3 days if you want to combine game drives with other activities.</p><p data-end="1986" data-start="1889" dir="auto">UWA lists Murchison Falls among Uganda's 10 national parks.<span data-content-reference-end="2112" data-content-reference-start="2093"><span data-state="closed"></span></span></p><h2 data-end="2051" data-section-id="qen3ge" data-start="1993" dir="auto">2. Queen Elizabeth National Park — for wildlife variety</h2><p data-end="2182" data-start="2095" dir="auto">Queen Elizabeth National Park is one of the more versatile choices for a Uganda safari.</p><p data-end="2329" data-start="2184" dir="auto">Its landscapes include savannah, wetlands, lakes and the famous <strong data-end="2267" data-start="2248">Kazinga Channel</strong>, where boat trips provide a different way of seeing wildlife.</p><p data-end="2526" data-start="2331" dir="auto">The wider park also includes the Ishasha sector, known for its tree-climbing lions. Seeing them is never guaranteed, however, so an itinerary should not be built around a single animal encounter.</p><p data-end="2724" data-start="2528" dir="auto">Queen Elizabeth works particularly well as part of a western Uganda circuit. It can be combined with destinations such as Kibale, Bwindi or the Rwenzori region depending on how much time you have.</p><p data-end="2822" data-start="2726" dir="auto"><strong data-end="2739" data-start="2726">Best for:</strong> first safaris, mixed wildlife experiences, boat trips and western Uganda circuits.</p><p data-end="2910" data-start="2824" dir="auto"><strong data-end="2837" data-start="2824">Plan for:</strong> 2–3 days for a useful introduction, longer if combining several sectors.</p><h2 data-end="2988" data-section-id="1yy7y4y" data-start="2917" dir="auto">3. Bwindi Impenetrable National Park — for mountain gorilla trekking</h2>If your main reason for visiting Uganda is <strong data-end="3096" data-start="3075">mountain gorillas</strong>, Bwindi belongs near the top of your itinerary.<p data-end="3421" data-start="3146" dir="auto">The forest is not a conventional drive-through safari destination. Gorilla trekking involves walking through forest terrain with trained guides and rangers, and the experience can range from relatively manageable to physically demanding depending on the group and conditions.</p><p data-end="3603" data-start="3423" dir="auto">Bwindi also has several trekking sectors, so where you stay matters. A lodge that looks close to Bwindi on a booking website may not necessarily be close to the trailhead you need.</p><h3 data-end="3645" data-section-id="uqvmnx" data-start="3605" dir="auto">How much does gorilla trekking cost?</h3><p data-end="3935" data-start="3647" dir="auto">UWA announced a new low-season permit structure in 2026. During <strong data-end="3738" data-start="3711">April, May and November</strong>, gorilla tracking permits are listed at <strong data-end="3848" data-start="3779">US$600 for foreign non-residents and US$500 for foreign residents</strong>. These discounted permits cannot be rescheduled.<span data-content-reference-end="4344" data-content-reference-start="4327"><span data-state="closed"></span></span></p><p data-end="4202" data-start="3937" dir="auto">UWA has also announced rates taking effect <strong data-end="3998" data-start="3980">1 January 2027</strong>, including a standard gorilla-tracking rate of <strong data-end="4163" data-start="4046">US$800 for foreign non-residents, US$700 for foreign residents and UGX300,000 for East African Community visitors</strong>.<span data-content-reference-end="4591" data-content-reference-start="4574"><span data-state="closed"></span></span></p><p data-end="4311" data-start="4204" dir="auto">Those figures cover the permit itself, not your transport, accommodation, meals, tips and other trip costs.</p><p data-end="4381" data-start="4313" dir="auto"><strong data-end="4326" data-start="4313">Best for:</strong> travellers whose priority is seeing mountain gorillas.</p><p data-end="4494" data-start="4383" dir="auto"><strong data-end="4396" data-start="4383">Plan for:</strong> advance permit arrangements, suitable accommodation and transport to the correct trekking sector.</p><h2 data-end="4558" data-section-id="1qj04ih" data-start="4501" dir="auto">4. Kibale National Park — for chimpanzees and primates</h2><p data-end="4753" data-start="4602" dir="auto">Kibale is the park to consider if your idea of wildlife is less “lion on the horizon” and more <strong data-end="4752" data-start="4697">chimpanzee moving through the trees above your head</strong>.</p><p data-end="4895" data-start="4755" dir="auto">The park is particularly associated with chimpanzee tracking and habituation experiences, alongside its wider collection of forest primates.</p><p data-end="4998" data-start="4897" dir="auto">That makes Kibale a natural companion to Queen Elizabeth National Park or a western Uganda itinerary.</p><p data-end="5235" data-start="5000" dir="auto">Chimpanzee trekking is also substantially different from a savannah game drive. You spend much of the experience walking through forest, following signs and listening for animals rather than simply driving from one sighting to another.</p><p data-end="5653" data-start="5237" dir="auto">UWA's 2026 tariff announcement lists chimpanzee tracking at Kibale at <strong data-end="5444" data-start="5307">US$300 for foreign non-residents, US$200 for foreign residents and UGX180,000 for East African Community visitors from 1 January 2027</strong>. A lower-season rate of <strong data-end="5538" data-start="5469">US$200 for foreign non-residents and US$150 for foreign residents</strong> applies during April, May and November under the announced 2026 arrangement.<span data-content-reference-end="6173" data-content-reference-start="6156"><span data-state="closed"></span></span></p><p data-end="5728" data-start="5655" dir="auto"><strong data-end="5668" data-start="5655">Best for:</strong> chimpanzees, primates, forest walks and nature enthusiasts.</p><p data-end="5877" data-start="5730" dir="auto"><strong data-end="5743" data-start="5730">Plan for:</strong> comfortable walking shoes, rain protection and enough time for the forest experience rather than treating it like a quick photo stop.</p><h2 data-end="5941" data-section-id="jow1ge" data-start="5884" dir="auto">5. Kidepo Valley National Park — for remote wilderness</h2>Kidepo is the choice for travellers who want Uganda to feel genuinely remote.<p data-end="6262" data-start="6064" dir="auto">Located in north-eastern Uganda, Kidepo offers expansive landscapes and a very different atmosphere from the more frequently visited western parks. The journey itself becomes part of the experience.</p><p data-end="6323" data-start="6264" dir="auto">That remoteness comes with a price: <strong data-end="6322" data-start="6300">time and logistics</strong>.</p><p data-end="6584" data-start="6325" dir="auto">You need to think seriously about transport, fuel, vehicle reliability, accommodation and the amount of time available. Trying to squeeze Kidepo into an already crowded itinerary can turn an exciting wilderness trip into a long exercise in watching the clock.</p><p data-end="6670" data-start="6586" dir="auto"><strong data-end="6599" data-start="6586">Best for:</strong> remote wilderness, scenery, photography and travellers with more time.</p><p data-end="6736" data-start="6672" dir="auto"><strong data-end="6685" data-start="6672">Plan for:</strong> several days rather than a rushed overnight visit.</p><p data-end="6849" data-start="6738" dir="auto">Kidepo Valley is one of the 10 national parks officially listed by UWA.<span data-content-reference-end="7489" data-content-reference-start="7470"><span data-state="closed"></span></span></p><h2 data-end="6909" data-section-id="zuk92i" data-start="6856" dir="auto">6. Lake Mburo National Park — for a shorter safari</h2>Lake Mburo is one of the more practical choices when you want a wildlife experience without committing to a much longer journey into western or northern Uganda.<p data-end="7294" data-start="7117" dir="auto">The park offers savannah scenery and wildlife experiences around the lake and surrounding landscapes. Its location also makes it useful as part of a trip towards western Uganda.</p><p data-end="7563" data-start="7296" dir="auto">Lake Mburo can be particularly interesting for travellers looking for activities beyond a conventional vehicle-based game drive. However, rules and availability for activities such as walking or cycling should be confirmed with UWA or your operator before travelling.</p><p data-end="7653" data-start="7565" dir="auto"><strong data-end="7578" data-start="7565">Best for:</strong> shorter safaris, road trips and travellers heading towards western Uganda.</p><p data-end="7751" data-start="7655" dir="auto"><strong data-end="7668" data-start="7655">Plan for:</strong> 1–2 nights for a compact visit, or longer if combining it with other destinations.</p><h2 data-end="7831" data-section-id="1e8tcrd" data-start="7758" dir="auto">7. Mgahinga Gorilla National Park — for gorillas, volcanoes and hiking</h2>Mgahinga is small compared with some of Uganda's larger parks, but its setting is anything but ordinary.<p data-end="8110" data-start="7983" dir="auto">The park sits within the Virunga landscape and combines <strong data-end="8109" data-start="8039">mountain forests, volcanic scenery and gorilla-related experiences</strong>.</p><p data-end="8211" data-start="8112" dir="auto">It is a particularly interesting choice for someone who wants wildlife and hiking in the same trip.</p><p data-end="8418" data-start="8213" dir="auto">Mountain conditions can change quickly, so proper footwear, rain protection and suitable clothing matter. Trekking should be approached as a physical outdoor activity rather than a leisurely forest stroll.</p><p data-end="8492" data-start="8420" dir="auto"><strong data-end="8433" data-start="8420">Best for:</strong> gorillas, volcanoes, hiking and dramatic mountain scenery.</p><p data-end="8592" data-start="8494" dir="auto"><strong data-end="8507" data-start="8494">Plan for:</strong> appropriate fitness, weather protection and a sensible itinerary with recovery time.</p><h2 data-end="8669" data-section-id="81js3i" data-start="8599" dir="auto">8. Rwenzori Mountains National Park — for serious mountain trekking</h2>The Rwenzori Mountains offer one of Uganda's most demanding outdoor experiences.<p data-end="8925" data-start="8797" dir="auto">This is not the park to visit if you simply want to jump out of a vehicle, take a photograph and be back at your hotel by lunch.</p><p data-end="9170" data-start="8927" dir="auto">Multi-day Rwenzori treks involve altitude, changing weather, difficult terrain and considerable physical preparation. Depending on the route, trekkers need to consider equipment, food, accommodation, porters, guides and emergency arrangements.</p><p data-end="9285" data-start="9172" dir="auto">The reward is a mountain environment unlike Uganda's savannah parks, including spectacular high-altitude scenery.</p><p data-end="9381" data-start="9287" dir="auto"><strong data-end="9300" data-start="9287">Best for:</strong> experienced hikers, mountaineers and travellers looking for a serious adventure.</p><p data-end="9469" data-start="9383" dir="auto"><strong data-end="9396" data-start="9383">Plan for:</strong> multiple days, physical preparation and a reputable guiding arrangement.</p><p data-end="9570" data-start="9471" dir="auto">UWA lists Rwenzori Mountains among Uganda's national parks.<span data-content-reference-end="10620" data-content-reference-start="10601"><span data-state="closed"></span></span></p><h2 data-end="9639" data-section-id="1sghi92" data-start="9577" dir="auto">9. Mount Elgon National Park — for hiking in eastern Uganda</h2>Mount Elgon provides another mountain option, but the experience differs considerably from the Rwenzori.<p data-end="9915" data-start="9791" dir="auto">The park is centred on Mount Elgon in eastern Uganda, with forest, highland environments and extensive hiking opportunities.</p><p data-end="10060" data-start="9917" dir="auto">It can also fit naturally into a broader eastern Uganda trip, particularly if you are exploring the region around Mbale and nearby attractions.</p><p data-end="10213" data-start="10062" dir="auto">Weather on the mountain can differ from conditions in lower-lying towns, so don't pack purely according to the forecast you saw before leaving Kampala.</p><p data-end="10284" data-start="10215" dir="auto"><strong data-end="10228" data-start="10215">Best for:</strong> hiking, highland scenery and eastern Uganda road trips.</p><p data-end="10372" data-start="10286" dir="auto"><strong data-end="10299" data-start="10286">Plan for:</strong> a suitable guide, proper hiking footwear and weather-resistant clothing.</p><h2 data-end="10451" data-section-id="13os10f" data-start="10379" dir="auto">10. Semuliki National Park — for forest, birds and unusual landscapes</h2>Semuliki is one of Uganda's more unusual national parks.<p data-end="10860" data-start="10555" dir="auto">Located in western Uganda near the Rwenzori Mountains, it protects a lowland tropical forest with strong ecological links to the Congo Basin. UWA describes it as the easternmost extension of the Ituri Forest and highlights its distinctive Central African character.<span data-content-reference-end="12159" data-content-reference-start="12140"><span data-state="closed"></span></span></p><p data-end="11014" data-start="10862" dir="auto">The park is particularly interesting for <strong data-end="11013" data-start="10903">birding, forest walks and visitors who want something different from the country's classic savannah safari</strong>.</p><p data-end="11132" data-start="11016" dir="auto">Semuliki also has hot springs and a landscape that feels markedly different from Queen Elizabeth or Murchison Falls.</p><p data-end="11221" data-start="11134" dir="auto"><strong data-end="11147" data-start="11134">Best for:</strong> birdwatchers, forest enthusiasts and travellers exploring western Uganda.</p><p data-end="11338" data-start="11223" dir="auto"><strong data-end="11236"
