<p> <span data-contrast="auto">So, the recent </span><a href="https://nymynet.com/tiktok-ban-in-the-u-s-a-national-security-threat-or-political-power-play/"><span data-contrast="none">TikTok ban</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> left many Americans scrambling for alternative platforms to continue their online ventures and exploits. Many other social media platforms have capitalized on this to accommodate new users, including, but not limited to creating tools that give a familiar feeling and user experience as what they had in TikTok.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">Instagram for instance has announced launch of a standalone video editing app set to very much replace TikTok's CapCut soon. And indeed, the development has come right when TikTok's U.S. operations future is rather bleak, particularly the ban causing a vacuum that competitors are eager to fill.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto"><em>On Sunday, the same day that TikTok was temporarily banned in the U.S., Instagram head Adam Mosseri announced a new app called Edits, a video-editing app that could take the place of the ByteDance-owned video-editing app CapCut — which was also banned alongside TikTok</em>. </span><a href="https://nypost.com/2025/01/21/tech/all-the-changes-coming-to-instagram-soon-as-tiktok-refugees-scramble/?utm_source=chatgpt.com"><span data-contrast="none">The New York Post.</span></a><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><h6><b>What will Edits do?</b><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></h6><p><span data-contrast="auto">Instagram’s Edits will provide its creators with a suite of tools for making and editing videos directly on their phones. during the announcement by Mosseri, the app isn't for those looking for templates or even those who use desktop apps, but rather for creators who make short-form content on their phones. Some of its features will include:</span></p><p><b>Editing Tools </b><span data-contrast="auto">like video cutting, AI-powered animations, green screen effects and even adding video overlays...talk about pro video editing!</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><b>Audio Enhancements. </b><span data-contrast="auto">The application also offers audio abilities like improving voice clarity, reducing background noises and generating customizable captions.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><b>Creative Elements </b><span data-contrast="auto">like variable fonts, sound and voice effects, video filters and stickers too.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><b>Performance Insights. </b><span data-contrast="auto">The app is set to provide a live insights dashboard with engagement and skip rate metrics, which has been a notable win for many content creators in managing content engagement and strategies.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">That’s not all, Edits will also allow its users to export videos without watermarks and share them across various platforms, including Instagram itself.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">Many creators have applauded the app for its promised comprehensive features, commending it as a timely alternative to TikTok's uncertain future.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">Others, however, question Instagram’s motives, referencing the platform’s tendency to copy and replicate features from other platforms.  I mean, we have all seen Instagram’s Threads app looking strikingly similar to X/twitter, or Instagram reels having that TikTok shortform content flair...</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">To put it plainly, </span><a href="https://nymynet.com/tiktok-ban-in-the-u-s-a-national-security-threat-or-political-power-play/"><span data-contrast="none">TikTok’s recent shutdown in the U.S.</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> under national security concerns left about 170 million American users looking for </span><a href="https://info.ssegu.com/tiktok-is-dying-7-big-apps-its-users-are-running-to/"><span data-contrast="none">TikTok alternatives,</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> not only disrupting the routines of many content creators and consumers but also providing a gap, and opening for the competing companies to capitalize on some of the wondering users. Instagram's Edits thus comes out as a calculated response to this opportunity, aiming to capture and accommodate some of the “TikTok Refugees.”</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p>that said, for now, Edits is available for free pre-order download from the App Store, and won’t officially launch until February, plus the first version of the app will be “incomplete,” though it will be updated over time--said Mosseri</p><p>The App Store lists an expected availability date of March 13.</p><h6><b>Bluesky's “Trending Videos”</b><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></h6><p><span data-contrast="auto">Not to be left behind, Bluesky, the decentralized social network, has also introduced its own TikTok-like feature they are named “Trending Videos.” this addition allows users to scroll through a feed of short-form videos, which to many is a replica of TikTok’s swipe experience. </span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">By integrating customizable video feeds and a dedicated explore section, Bluesky seems to be garnering for TikTok users seeking alternatives to their familiar user experience. On top of that, Bluesky’s emphasis on user control and content curation differentiates it from other social media platforms.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">That said, while Instagram and Bluesky’s actions demonstrate agility in seizing market opportunities, they also put them in direct fire when it comes to questions of ethics in capitalizing on competitors’ misfortune.
