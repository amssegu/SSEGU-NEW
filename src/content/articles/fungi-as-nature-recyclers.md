This decomposition process recycles nutrients back into the ecosystem, making them available for other organisms, including plants, which in turn support a wide range of animal life. They secrete enzymes outside their bodies, which break down large organic molecules into smaller, more manageable compounds. By decomposing organic materials, fungi release carbon dioxide back into the atmosphere as part of their respiration. However, some fungi also form partnerships with plants that result in more carbon being stored in the soil, reducing the amount of carbon in the atmosphere and potentially mitigating climate change impacts.</p><pSegoe UI", Roboto, "Helvetica Neue", Arial, "Noto Sans", sans-serif, "Apple Color Emoji", "Segoe UI Emoji", "Segoe UI Symbol", "Noto Color Emoji"; font-size: 16px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;'><span>Q7: Are fungi involved in any other unique ecological roles or interactions?</span></p><pSegoe UI", Roboto, "Helvetica Neue", Arial, "Noto Sans", sans-serif, "Apple Color Emoji", "Segoe UI Emoji", "Segoe UI Symbol", "Noto Color Emoji"; font-size: 16px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;'>Yes, besides decomposition and symbiotic relationships, some fungi are key players in biocontrol.
