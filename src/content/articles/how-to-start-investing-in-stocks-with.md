  <p>
    Many people believe you need thousands of dollars to begin investing in the stock market.
    In reality, modern investing platforms have made it possible to start with as little as $100.
  </p>

  <p>
    Thanks to commission-free trading and fractional shares, beginners can now build diversified
    portfolios directly from their smartphones without large upfront capital.
  </p>

  <p>
    This step-by-step guide explains how to start investing with a small budget while reducing risk
    and building long-term financial habits.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>Step 1: Choose a Beginner-Friendly Brokerage Account</h2>

  <p>
    Traditional brokers once charged high trading commissions that made small investments impractical.
    Today, many online brokers offer commission-free investing and fractional share purchasing.
  </p>

  <p>
    Fractional shares allow investors to buy portions of expensive stocks instead of needing enough
    money to purchase a full share.
  </p>

    </li>

  <h2>Step 2: Pick an Investment Strategy</h2>

  <p>
    Diversification is one of the most important principles in investing.
    Spreading money across multiple assets can help reduce risk over time.
  </p>

  <h3>Strategy A: Invest in a Broad Market ETF</h3>

  <p>
    Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) are one of the easiest ways for beginners to invest.
    An ETF contains a collection of stocks bundled together into one investment.
  </p>

  <p>
    Instead of buying shares of a single company, investors gain exposure to hundreds of businesses
    through one purchase.
  </p>

  <h4>Examples of Popular ETFs</h4>

  <ul>
    <li>
      <strong>VTI:</strong> Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF
    </li>

    <li>
      <strong>VOO:</strong> Vanguard S&amp;P 500 ETF
    </li>

  </ul>

  <p>
    This strategy is often considered beginner-friendly because it spreads risk across many companies.
  </p>

  <p>
    Learn more about ETFs here:
  </p>

  <p>
    <a href="https://investor.vanguard.com/investment-products/etfs" rel="nofollow noopener" target="_blank">
      Vanguard ETF Information
    </a>
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h3>Strategy B: Build a Small Fractional Share Portfolio</h3>

  <p>
    Investors who prefer owning individual companies can use fractional shares to create
    a diversified mini-portfolio.
  </p>

  <p>
    For example, instead of placing the full $100 into one company, you could divide it among
    several well-known businesses.
  </p>

  <h4>Example Allocation</h4>

  <ul>
    <li>$20 in Company A</li>
    <li>$20 in Company B</li>
    <li>$20 in Company C</li>
    <li>$20 in Company D</li>
    <li>$20 in Company E</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    This approach can reduce the impact of one stock performing poorly.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>Step 3: Place Your First Investment</h2>

  <p>
    Once your brokerage account is approved and linked to your bank account,
    placing your first trade is relatively simple.
  </p>

  <ol>
    <li>Search for the stock or ETF ticker symbol.</li>
    <li>Select the option to invest by dollar amount.</li>
    <li>Enter your investment amount.</li>
    <li>Review the order details carefully.</li>
    <li>Submit the trade.</li>
  </ol>

  <p>
    Beginners should take time to understand the difference between market orders,
    limit orders, and long-term investing strategies before trading frequently.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>Step 4: Automate Your Investments</h2>

  <p>
    Building wealth is usually driven by consistency rather than one-time investments.
  </p>

  <p>
    Many brokers allow users to automate recurring investments directly from a bank account.
    Even investing small amounts monthly can grow significantly over time through compounding.
  </p>

  <h3>Consider Dividend Reinvestment</h3>

  <p>
    Some ETFs and stocks pay dividends. Investors can activate a Dividend Reinvestment Plan (DRIP)
    to automatically reinvest those earnings into additional shares.
  </p>

  <p>
    This can gradually increase portfolio value without requiring additional manual trades.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>Important Reminder About Risk</h2>

  <p>
    All investments involve risk, including the possibility of losing money.
    Stock prices can rise and fall due to market conditions, economic events, and company performance.
  </p>

  <p>
    New investors should focus on long-term goals, diversification, and steady contributions
    rather than short-term speculation.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>Final Thoughts</h2>

  <p>
    Starting with $100 may seem small, but developing consistent investing habits early
    can make a significant difference over time.
  </p>

  <p>
    With modern brokerage apps, fractional shares, and commission-free investing,
    nearly anyone can begin building a portfolio regardless of budget size.
  </p>

  <p>
    The key is to start carefully, stay informed, and continue investing consistently.
  </p>
