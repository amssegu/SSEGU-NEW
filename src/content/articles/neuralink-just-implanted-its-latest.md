<p> <span data-contrast="auto">Neuralink, the tech company founded by Elon Musk has achieved yet another breakthrough with the 3</span><span data-contrast="auto">rd</span><span data-contrast="auto"> implantation of its brain-computer interface (BCI) device, the Neuralink brain chip.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">Elon Musk’s brain-child, Neuralink, is making strides toward a future where human brains and computers are seamlessly connected. At the <a href="https://www.ces.tech/">CES 2025</a> event, Musk confirmed that the company has successfully implanted its brain-computer interface (BCI) device in a third patient — and that it’s just the beginning. He stated,</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><i>‘we’ve now got three humans with Neuralinks implanted, and they’re all working well.”</i><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">Further, he revealed plans to perform 20 to 30 more implants this year 2025.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">Neuralink's BCI tech involves a coin-sized device implanted in the brain, equipped with thin electrodes that detect and interpret neural signals. These signals are then wirelessly transmitted to external devices, enabling users to control computers, smartphones, or even robotic limbs through that alone. The procedure itself is minimally invasive, involving a specialized surgical robot that carefully inserts electrodes while avoiding blood vessels to reduce risk. We further discuss this in, </span><a href="https://nymynet.com/a-deep-dive-into-elon-musks-neuralink-technology/"><span data-contrast="none">A deep Dive into Elon Musk’s Neuralink Technology.</span></a><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">The first implant occurred in January 2024 with patient Noland Arbaugh who had been paralyzed from the shoulders down following a driving accident. Feedback from Noland indicated the ability to interact with digital devices using his thoughts, significantly enhancing the quality of his life.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p><span data-contrast="auto">However, this first design faced some electrodes detaching over time, which led to a refinement of the original design and algorithms.</span><span data-ccp-props="{}"> </span></p><p>From here, neuralink registered two studies with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA). The first, known as the PRIME study (Precise Robotically Implanted Brain-Computer Interface), involved five patients with paralysis to enable them to control external devices through their thoughts. The second study, Convoy, focuses on three patients and aims to facilitate control of assistive devices such as robotic arms all via the BCI.</p><p><span data-contrast="auto">Neuralink's successful implantation of its BCI device in a third patient shows progress in integrating neurotechnology into human augmentation.
