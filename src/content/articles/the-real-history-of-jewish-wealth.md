Judaism and Wealth</span></h4><p dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">Judaism doesn't view poverty as a sign of righteousness. Instead, it promotes the idea that everyone should avoid poverty and be gainfully employed. This fundamental value fosters a strong work ethic and personal responsibility. Jewish people also believe in giving, with 10% to 20% of wages going towards religious and charitable causes. However, they don't rely on charity for upliftment, aiming to avoid becoming a burden on the community. This mindset builds a solid foundation for success.</span></p><br><h4 dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">2. European Jews Couldn't Own Land</span></h4><p dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">Anti-Semitism predates World War II. Jewish people faced significant challenges, including being prohibited from owning land in Europe since medieval times. However, they found alternative ways to build wealth, such as becoming merchants and traders. Religious Opportunity and Financial Roles</span></h4><p dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">In the Middle Ages, Christians viewed charging interest as a sin, leaving financial job opportunities open to the Jewish community, who did not share this belief. Jewish people became prominent in money lending businesses, a precursor to many modern stereotypes.</span></p><br><h4 dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">4. The First Banks</span></h4><p dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">Transitioning from money lending, Jewish people opened the first banks in Europe. They managed transactions, transfers, and financial services, continuing to lend money and charge interest. Their success in finance fueled growth but also led to jealousy and anti-Semitic sentiment.</span></p><br><h4 dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">5. The Most Educated Religious Group</span></h4><p dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">In 2016, the Pew Center identified Jewish people as the most educated religious group globally, with an average of 13.4 years of schooling. This education translates to higher starting salaries and improved financial status immediately after university.</span></p><br><h4 dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">6. Community Support</span></h4><p dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">Jewish people have a deep pride in sharing their wealth to benefit their community. This mutual aid mentality ensures that each community member is supported. Judaic law instructs that the poor are to be respected and protected, fostering a strong focus on uplifting the entire community.</span></p><br><h4 dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">7. Good Business Practices</span></h4><p dir="ltr"><span face="Arial, sans-serif">The Torah and Jewish philosophers emphasize honest business practices.
