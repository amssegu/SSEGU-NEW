Bahá'í Faith - 7 Million Adherents</span></span></h2><p dir="ltr"><span><span>The Bahá'í Faith was founded by Bahá'u'lláh, a Persian man, in the 19th century. This religion believes in the unity of all religions, viewing religious history as a series of divine messengers—Krishna, Buddha, Moses, Jesus, Muhammad—all of whom established religions suited to the needs of their times. Followers of the Bahá'í Faith practice prayer and worship one God, who they believe is the same for all humans. Their rituals are simple and often occur in homes or centers, with seven Bahá'í Houses of Worship around the world.</span></span></p><span><br></span><h2 dir="ltr"><span><span>9. Judaism - 14 Million Adherents</span></span></h2><p dir="ltr"><span><span>Judaism's roots trace back to 832 BC with the construction of Solomon's Temple in Jerusalem. Abraham is considered the father of the Jewish people. When his descendants were enslaved in Egypt, God instructed Moses to lead the Exodus. Judaism holds a rich history and tradition, with an emphasis on following the commandments given by God.</span></span></p><span><br></span><h2 dir="ltr"><span><span>8. Spiritism - 15 Million Adherents</span></span></h2><p dir="ltr"><span><span>Founded in the early 19th century by French educator Allan Kardec, Spiritism differs from most religions as it does not see its founder as a prophet. Kardec wrote five books based on research and testimonies of the spiritual world. Spiritism teaches the same values as Christianity but does not recognize Jesus as the Son of God. Followers believe humans are immortal spirits inhabiting physical bodies to improve morally and intellectually.</span></span></p><span><br></span><h2 dir="ltr"><span><span>7. Sikhism - 23 Million Adherents</span></span></h2><p dir="ltr"><span><span>Sikhism was founded by Guru Nanak in the 16th century in Pakistan. Sikhs believe in one supreme reality, God, known as Ik Onkar. They pray through hymns and meditation and emphasize selfless service. Unique practices include the prohibition of cutting hair for those who have undergone the Amrit initiation ceremony.</span></span></p><span><br></span><h2 dir="ltr"><span><span>6. African Traditional Religion - 100 Million Adherents</span></span></h2><p dir="ltr"><span><span>African traditional religions are diverse but share common concepts such as belief in a supreme creator, spirits, ancestor veneration, and magic. They worship supernatural spirits, including ancestors, who serve as intermediaries between humans and God. Religious rituals often involve animal sacrifices and libations.</span></span></p><span><br></span><h2 dir="ltr"><span><span>5. Buddhism - 376 Million Adherents</span></span></h2><p dir="ltr"><span><span>Buddhism was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, who was born in Lumbini (now part of Nepal) in 563 BC. After renouncing his luxurious life, he discovered the suffering of others and attained enlightenment through intense meditation. Buddha's teachings focus on reaching Nirvana and escaping the cycle of rebirth. His teachings have inspired millions to follow his path to enlightenment.</span></span></p><span><br></span><h2 dir="ltr"><span><span>4. Chinese Traditional Religion - 394 Million Adherents</span></span></h2><p dir="ltr"><span><span>Chinese traditional religion dates back to 206 BC during the Han Dynasty. It involves the worship of gods and spirits, viewed as forces within nature. Four key concepts include Tian (heaven), Qi (breath of substance), Jingzu (ancestor veneration), and Bao Ying (moral reciprocity). These concepts cover the spiritual, cosmological, and moral aspects of life.</span></span></p><span><br></span><h2 dir="ltr"><span><span>3. Hinduism - 900 Million Adherents</span></span></h2><p dir="ltr"><span><span>Hinduism is one of the oldest religions, originating from various Indian cultures and traditions. Its complex concept of God varies among individuals and traditions. Hindus worship a Supreme Being manifested as Vishnu, Brahma, Shiva, and Shakti. The religion emphasizes the four proper aims of human life: Dharma (duties), Artha (prosperity), Kama (emotion), and Moksha (liberation).</span></span></p><span><br></span><h2 dir="ltr"><span><span>2. Islam - 1.5 Billion Adherents</span></span></h2><p dir="ltr"><span><span>Islam was founded in 610 CE when Muhammad received revelations from God through Archangel Gabriel, recorded in the Quran. Islam strictly believes in one God, Allah, who is almighty and created the universe. Muslims believe Muhammad is the final prophet, and they follow the teachings of the Quran and Hadith to lead their lives.</span></span></p><span><br></span><h2 dir="ltr"><span><span>1. Christianity - 2.1 Billion Adherents</span></span></h2><p dir="ltr"><span><span>Christianity is the largest religion, with 2.1 billion adherents. Christians believe Jesus Christ, born in 4 BC, is the Son of God who brought salvation to humanity. The Bible, which chronicles Jesus' life and teachings, serves as the foundation of Christian faith. Central to Christianity is the belief in the Trinity: one God in three persons—the Father, the Son (Jesus Christ), and the Holy Spirit. The major denominations are Roman Catholicism, Eastern Orthodoxy, and Protestantism.</span></span></p><p dir="ltr"><span><span>Before this article, we spoke to people from these religions to ensure accurate representation.
