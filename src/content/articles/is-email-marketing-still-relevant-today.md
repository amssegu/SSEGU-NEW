<p> <strong>Emails...</strong> If you’re reading this, chances are you have an email account—or maybe even two or three. Gmail, Yahoo Mail, Outlook, you name it. And if you have an email, then at some point, you've probably subscribed to a newsletter, signed up for a freebie, or received promotional emails from businesses trying to catch your attention and that, my friend, is email marketing--which brings me to today's article, how do you use email marketing effectively? How can you ensure your emails don’t end up ignored, unopened, or worse—marked as spam? Well, let me show you how you can use email marketing to grow your business.</p><h5><strong>Why Email Marketing Still Works</strong></h5><p>Social media has taken up marketing trends, that's for sure; so if you are wondering if <em>email marketing is still relevant today,</em> the answer is a resounding <em>yes!</em></p><ul><li><strong>Direct Connection</strong> – Unlike social media, where algorithms dictate who sees your posts, emails land straight in your audience’s inbox.</li><li><strong>Higher ROI</strong> – Studies show that for every $1 spent on email marketing, businesses see an average return of $36.</li><li><strong>Personalized Communication</strong> – You can design emails based on customer behavior, preferences, and purchase history.</li><li><strong>Automation &amp; Scalability</strong> – With email automation, you can reach thousands (or even millions) of people without lifting a finger.</li></ul><p>Now that we know <em>why</em> email marketing is still a relevant player, let’s get into the <em>how.</em></p><h5><strong>1. Build a Quality Email List</strong></h5><p>Your email marketing efforts are only as good as your email list. If you’re sending emails to people who don’t care about your business, you’re wasting time and resources, so your first goal would be to build a strong and relevant email list.</p><h6><strong>a) Offer Value in Exchange for Emails</strong></h6><p>People don’t give away their email addresses for free, and truth to point I am sure the only time you gave up your email to subscribe to a newsletter was because that particular site had something that interested you. You need to entice them with something valuable, for example:</p><ul><li>A free eBook, guide, or checklist</li><li>Discount codes or exclusive deals</li><li>Access to webinars or premium content</li><li>Free trials or samples</li></ul><h6><strong>b) Use Pop-Ups &amp; Sign-Up Forms</strong></h6><p>Place well-designed sign-up forms on your website, blog, and social media pages. However, while pop-ups work well, they should be strategically timed. you could try showing the pop-up as the user is exiting that particular page instead of annoying interruptions.</p><h6><strong>c) Avoid Buying Email Lists</strong></h6><p>Buying emails might seem like a shortcut, but it’s a terrible idea since most of these emails are either inactive or uninterested which leads to low engagement rates and spam complaints.</p><h5><strong>2. Write Emails That Get Opened</strong></h5><p>Your email campaign is useless if no one opens your emails, so try as much as possible to design emails that are hard to ignore.</p><h6><strong>a) Write Irresistible Subject Lines</strong></h6><p>Your subject line is the first thing recipients see, so make it count. Some best practices include:</p><ul><li>Keeping it short and sweet</li><li>Creating curiosity, because we humans are simply curious creatures... “You’re missing out on this…" could be a good example. I for one would very much like to know what I am missing out on.</li><li>Personalizing. Just as we are curious, we also enjoy feeling special. You this in your email campaign by offering personalized emails, for example, you could include the name used when subscribing to your site...“Hey [Name], here’s a special deal for you”</li></ul><h6><strong>b) Nail the Email Preview Text</strong></h6><p>The preview text (the short snippet seen before opening an email) should complement the subject line. Use it to build curiosity or reinforce urgency so that the receiver is engrossed enough to open the email.</p><h6><strong>c) Send at the Right Time</strong></h6><p>Timing matters! Studies suggest that emails sent on <strong>Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday mornings</strong> have the highest open rates. However, test different times to see what works best for your audience.</p><h5><strong>3. Personalize &amp; Segment Your Audience</strong></h5><p>Generic mass emails don’t work anymore, because, as I mentioned earlier we humans like being and feeling special...personalization is key to engagement.</p><h6><strong>a) Segment Your List</strong></h6><p>Break your audience into groups based on:</p><ul><li>Purchase history</li><li>Location</li><li>Interests</li><li>Engagement level</li></ul><p>For example, if someone just signed up, send them a welcome email series. If they’re loyal customers, send exclusive offers.</p><h6><strong>b) Use Dynamic Content</strong></h6><p>Emails that address recipients by name and recommend products based on their interests perform much better. Use email marketing tools to automate this.</p><h5><strong>4. Automate Your Email Campaigns</strong></h5><p>Automation saves time and increases efficiency.</p><ul><li><strong>Welcome Series</strong> – Introduce new subscribers to your brand with 3–5 well-spaced emails.</li><li><strong>Abandoned Cart Emails</strong> – Remind customers of items they left in their cart.</li><li><strong>Re-Engagement Campaigns</strong> – Win back inactive subscribers with personalized offers.</li><li><strong>Post-Purchase Follow-Ups</strong> – Thank customers and offer upsells or referrals.</li></ul><h5><strong>5. Keep Your Emails Engaging</strong></h5><p>No one likes a boring email. Keep your emails interesting in any way you can manage. you could try:</p><ul><li>Using <strong>storytelling</strong> to connect with readers</li><li>Including <strong>interactive elements</strong> like polls, GIFs, or quizzes</li><li>Keeping paragraphs short and scannable</li><li>Adding a clear <strong>call-to-action (CTA)</strong> for example “Claim Your Discount Now” to try and have the receiver click and interact with the email.</li></ul><h5><strong>6. Optimize for Mobile Users</strong></h5><p>Over 60% of emails are opened on mobile devices, so make sure your emails</p><ul><li>Have a responsive design for both small and wider screens</li><li>Use short subject lines that won't get cut short on smartphone screens</li><li>Feature a single-column layout since this easily accommodates smaller screens that are more vertical than horizontal</li><li>Keep CTA buttons large and easy to tap</li></ul><h5><strong>7. Track &amp; Improve Performance</strong></h5><p>You won’t know what’s working unless you track results, so always pay attention to:</p><ul><li><strong>Open Rates</strong> – Indicates how engaging your subject lines are</li><li><strong>Click-through rates (CTR)</strong> – Show how compelling your content and CTAs are</li><li><strong>Conversion Rates</strong> – Measures how many readers take action</li><li><strong>Unsubscribe Rates</strong> – Helps you spot problems in your campaigns</li></ul><p>Use tools like <strong>Mailchimp, ConvertKit, ActiveCampaign,</strong> or <strong>HubSpot</strong> to track performance and optimize campaigns accordingly.</p><p>So, do you feel ready yet?</p><p>Email marketing is one of the most effective ways to grow your business—when done right. Focus on building a quality email list, designing irresistible messages, personalizing content, and automating campaigns for efficiency...it might take some time so keep testing and editing your approach, and you’ll see your business pick up eventually.</p><p>If you haven’t started email marketing yet, it’s time to build that email list.
