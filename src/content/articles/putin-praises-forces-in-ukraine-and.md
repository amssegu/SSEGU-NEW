<p><span>Russian President Vladimir Putin marked the 79th anniversary of Nazi Germany's defeat in World War II with a powerful display of military might, celebrating Russia's historic victory while delivering sharp criticism aimed at Western nations. Under the banner of Victory Day, Putin utilized the occasion to commend Russian forces engaged in Ukraine and to rebuke the West for its perceived role in global conflicts.</span></p><p></p><div><a href="https://images.getimg.ai/resize?url=https%3A%2F%2Fimg.getimg.ai%2Fgenerated%2Fimg-ja5bBdjCxJaNDC8whwITA.jpeg&amp;type=auto&amp;width=1080&amp;speed=5"></a></div><br><span><br></span><p></p><p><span>Putin, known for his strong stance on national identity and military prowess, has long held Victory Day as a cornerstone of his leadership. Despite the dwindling numbers of veterans from what Russia calls the Great Patriotic War, the event remains deeply revered, symbolizing Russia's strength and resilience.</span></p><p><span><br></span></p><p><span>In his address from Red Square, amidst an unusual May chill and occasional snow showers, Putin emphasized the unifying nature of Victory Day, rallying generations past and present towards a shared vision of a secure future for Russia. The grand military parade, featuring both historic and modern equipment, served as a testament to Russia's commitment to defending its interests.</span></p><p><span><br></span></p><p><span>However, Putin's rhetoric took a confrontational turn as he accused the West of exacerbating conflicts worldwide, including in Ukraine. Amid escalating tensions reminiscent of the Cold War era, Putin reiterated Russia's nuclear capabilities, issuing a stern warning against any perceived threats to its sovereignty.</span></p><p><span><br></span></p><p><span>The historical significance of World War II looms large in Russian consciousness, with the Soviet Union enduring immense losses that continue to reverberate through the nation's collective memory. From the devastating battles of Stalingrad to the siege of Leningrad, the war left an indelible mark on Russian society.</span></p><p><span><br></span></p><p><span>Since assuming power at the turn of the century, Putin has made it a priority to honor the sacrifices of those who fought in World War II, integrating military displays into the annual Victory Day celebrations. Despite controversies surrounding Russia's actions in Ukraine, thousands of soldiers, including veterans of recent conflicts, participated in this year's parade.</span></p><p><span><br></span></p><p><span>While notable absences, such as the ambassadors from the United States and the United Kingdom, underscored diplomatic tensions, several former Soviet states, and other allies stood in solidarity with Putin. The event served as a platform for Putin to underscore his personal connection to the war, invoking his father's wartime experiences as a testament to Russia's enduring spirit.</span></p><p><span><br></span></p><p><span>Putin's narrative, which seeks to portray Ukraine as harboring neo-Nazi elements, has drawn condemnation from Kyiv and its Western allies.
