  <!--Introduction-->
  <section>
    <p>
      Learning to read is one of the most exciting milestones in a child's early education. However, transitioning from simple alphabet sounds to reading complete words can sometimes feel daunting for young learners. The secret to bridging this gap lies in <strong>CVC words</strong> (Consonant-Vowel-Consonant) and sound blending!
    </p>
    <p>
      In this comprehensive guide, modeled after Miss V's engaging lesson, we break down simple 3-letter words into three distinct categories: <strong>Animals</strong>, <strong>Everyday Objects</strong>, and <strong>Action Words</strong>. By teaching kids to isolate each phonetic sound and blend them together, they build the confidence needed to become fluent readers.
    </p>
  </section>

  <!--Key Takeaways / Tips for Parents-->
  <section>
    <h3>Top Tips for Parents &amp; Teachers</h3>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Focus on Individual Sounds First:</strong> Say each letter sound clearly before trying to combine them (e.g., <em>/c/ ... /a/ ... /t/</em>).</li>
      <li><strong>Blend Slowly:</strong> Drag the sounds out so children can hear how they connect into a single word.</li>
      <li><strong>Keep Practices Short:</strong> Short 10-to-15-minute daily sessions keep energy and focus high.</li>
      <li><strong>Celebrate Progress:</strong> High fives and enthusiastic encouragement build reading confidence!</li>
    </ol>
