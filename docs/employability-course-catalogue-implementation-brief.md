# Course Catalogue: Employability & Skills Intelligence

## Product and implementation brief

**Prepared:** 22 November 2026\
**Primary audience:** Learners in Uganda\
**Secondary audience:** International learners\
**Initial content scope:** The existing 50-course shortlist\
**Suggested implementation:** Astro content collections with structured
Markdown records

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Product vision

Build a curated, career-oriented learning catalogue that helps people
make informed decisions about which skills to develop, which courses to
take, whether to use free or paid learning options, and what to do next.

This is not simply a directory of online courses. Each entry should
explain the learning outcome and its practical value:

-   What is the course about?
-   Who should take it, and what prerequisites are needed?
-   What skills will the learner acquire?
-   What work could those skills help them perform?
-   How relevant are the skills to Uganda and international
    opportunities?
-   What is available for free, and what does paying add?
-   Is the paid option worth the cost?
-   What should the learner study or build next?

**Core promise:** Help learners choose a sensible learning route and
turn learning into demonstrable skills---not promise that a course or
certificate guarantees employment.

## 2. Product principles

1.  **Employability before certificate collection.** Explain practical
    capability, role relevance and evidence of skill, not just the
    credential.
2.  **Evidence-led recommendations.** Distinguish provider facts,
    job-market evidence and editorial judgement.
3.  **Free access must be described accurately.** A free preview or
    audit is not the same as a fully free course.
4.  **Uganda-aware, internationally useful.** Check availability,
    payment, pricing and practical relevance for learners in Uganda
    while retaining international context.
5.  **No job guarantees.** Describe plausible pathways and additional
    requirements; never imply that completing a course guarantees a job,
    salary or promotion.
6.  **Provider pages are the source of truth.** Verify course
    availability, curriculum, prices, certificate terms, prerequisites
    and regional access against official sources.
7.  **Keep the catalogue selective and current.** A smaller,
    well-researched catalogue is better than a large collection of
    shallow or stale entries.

## 3. Audiences and use cases

### Primary learner groups

-   Beginners exploring a new field.
-   University and college students preparing for work.
-   Graduates seeking practical, marketable skills.
-   Working professionals seeking advancement or a career change.
-   Freelancers and aspiring independent workers.
-   Entrepreneurs and small-business operators.

### Main user journeys

**A. I know the skill I want** 1. Search for a course, provider, skill
or subject. 2. Filter by level, cost, category and credential. 3.
Compare relevant courses. 4. Review the full course profile. 5. Choose a
free or paid route. 6. Follow the recommended next step.

**B. I know the career I want, but not which course to take** 1. Browse
career pathways. 2. Choose a target role or career area. 3. See the
skills and foundations required. 4. Follow a staged learning sequence.
5. Complete practical projects. 6. Review further qualifications and
progression options.

**C. I have little or no budget** 1. Filter for genuinely free learning.
2. See what is included and what is restricted. 3. Identify free
certificates, badges or completion records. 4. See whether paying would
add meaningful value. 5. Follow a free-first route and build a
portfolio.

## 4. Scope

### Initial release

-   Searchable course catalogue.
-   Category and subject browsing.
-   Course cards and individual course detail pages.
-   Filters for level, cost/access model, provider and credential type.
-   Free-versus-paid comparison on applicable courses.
-   Career opportunities and skills sections.
-   Recommended next courses and progression.
-   Practical portfolio/project suggestions.
-   Source references and last-verified dates.
-   Uganda access and payment notes where verified.
-   Related courses and introductory career pathways.

### Subsequent enhancements

-   More detailed career pathways.
-   Career-goal discovery tool ("What can I become?").
-   Course comparison tool.
-   Learner accounts, saved courses and progress tracking.
-   Editorial/admin dashboard.
-   Automated checks for broken links and scheduled review reminders.
-   Job-posting evidence and skills-demand summaries.
-   Personalised recommendations based on existing skills and goals.

### Explicitly out of scope for the first release

-   Hosting or selling the courses.
-   Guaranteeing employment or income outcomes.
-   Issuing credentials on behalf of course providers.
-   Automatically claiming that a certificate is accredited or
    employer-recognised without evidence.
-   Building a full learning-management system.
-   Complex learner accounts or progress tracking before the catalogue
    itself is validated.

## 5. Standard course profile: required content

Every published course should use a consistent profile. Some fields may
be marked "not applicable" or "not verified", but missing research must
not be disguised as a fact.

### 5.1 Course identity

-   Course title.
-   Provider and delivery platform.
-   Category and subcategory.
-   Course level: beginner, intermediate or advanced.
-   Estimated duration and workload, where published.
-   Learning format: self-paced, instructor-led or cohort-based.
-   Prerequisites.
-   Required equipment, software or internet access.
-   Official course page and direct enrolment link.
-   Course availability/status and last-verified date.

### 5.2 Course overview

Write a clear, original explanation of what the course teaches. Cover:

-   Subject and purpose.
-   Main topics and modules.
-   Concepts the learner should understand.
-   Practical exercises, labs or projects.
-   Expected learning outcomes.
-   Important limitations: what the course does not teach.

Do not merely copy the provider's promotional description.

### 5.3 Intended learner

Explain:

-   Who should take the course.
-   Whether it is suitable for complete beginners.
-   Relevant existing knowledge or experience.
-   Who may benefit most.
-   Who should take a foundation course first.
-   Any prerequisites that could prevent a learner from succeeding.

### 5.4 Skills acquired

Separate skills into useful groups:

-   Technical or subject-specific skills.
-   Tools and software.
-   Practical workplace skills.
-   Transferable skills.

Where possible, describe the expected level as introductory exposure,
working knowledge or demonstrated practical competence. Do not claim
competence merely because a topic appears in the curriculum.

### 5.5 Career and employability prospects

For each course, research plausible uses of the skills:

-   Relevant job titles and entry-level roles.
-   Industries and types of organisations that use the skills.
-   Freelancing or self-employment services.
-   How the skills may support career advancement.
-   Common complementary skills employers request.
-   Experience, portfolio evidence or further qualifications usually
    needed.

Present roles as possible pathways, not guaranteed outcomes. Explain
where a course alone is insufficient.

### 5.6 Uganda and international relevance

Where evidence is available, cover:

-   Relevance to Ugandan employers and industries.
-   Relevant international or remote-work opportunities.
-   Local job titles and employer requirements.
-   Common skills requested alongside the course's subject.
-   Freelance-service potential.
-   Regional access, language, payment and connectivity considerations.
-   Whether a credential has specific recognition or value---and by
    whom.

Avoid broad statements such as "high demand" unless supported by dated,
relevant evidence.

### 5.7 Free learning option

Record:

-   Official free-access URL.
-   Whether the entire course or only selected material is free.
-   Whether videos, readings, labs and exercises are included.
-   Whether graded assessments are available.
-   Whether practical projects are included.
-   Whether a certificate, badge or statement is available at no cost.
-   Whether identity verification or payment is required.
-   Geographic restrictions or other limitations.

Use precise labels such as: - Free course and free credential. - Free
learning; paid certificate or exam. - Free audit; paid graded
work/certificate. - Free preview only. - Paid programme; financial aid
may be available (only if verified).

### 5.8 Paid learning option

Record:

-   Official enrolment URL.
-   Current price and currency, if visible.
-   Whether payment is one-off, per course, subscription-based or
    exam-based.
-   What payment unlocks.
-   Graded assignments, labs, projects or feedback.
-   Credential type and issuer.
-   Financial aid, scholarships or discounts.
-   Whether the payment route is accessible from Uganda.
-   Any additional costs, such as software, equipment or exam fees.

Prices must include a verification date and should not be converted into
UGX unless the exchange-rate source and date are recorded. If Uganda
checkout pricing cannot be verified, say so and direct the learner to
the provider's checkout.

### 5.9 Free-versus-paid assessment

Explain the practical differences, not just the price difference.

Answer: - Is the learning content itself different? - Are assessments or
projects restricted? - What credential does each route provide? - Does
paying add feedback, structure or support? - Is the paid credential
relevant to the target roles? - Can a learner achieve the main skill
outcome for free? - Under what circumstances is paying worthwhile?

End with one clear recommendation: - **Free is sufficient** --- the free
route provides enough learning and practice. - **Consider paying** ---
assessment, structure or credential offers meaningful additional
value. - **Paying is optional** --- useful convenience or recognition,
but not essential. - **Not recommended yet** --- the learner should
first establish foundations or confirm the career direction.

### 5.10 Upgrade and progression pathway

Recommend sensible next steps based on the learner's goal and current
level.

For each next step, include: - Skill or qualification to pursue. - Why
it follows this course. - What new capability it adds. - Suggested
course/provider and official link. - Whether it is free or paid. - Which
learner goal it supports: employment, freelancing, specialisation or
advancement.

Show alternative routes where appropriate. Do not force every learner
through the same sequence.

### 5.11 Portfolio and practical experience

Recommend concrete ways to demonstrate ability:

-   Project or assignment idea.
-   Skills demonstrated.
-   Expected output or deliverable.
-   Suggested tools.
-   Where the learner can publish or present the work.

Examples: - Data analytics: dashboard, cleaned dataset and written
analysis. - Web development: deployed websites with source code. -
Digital marketing: campaign plan, sample content and performance
report. - Project management: project charter, schedule, budget and risk
register. - Graphic design: curated portfolio with project explanations.

A certificate should not be presented as a substitute for practical
evidence where employers expect demonstrable work.

### 5.12 Editorial assessment

Assess the course using a consistent rubric:

-   Practical usefulness.
-   Beginner accessibility.
-   Portfolio potential.
-   Credential value.
-   Cost effectiveness.
-   Relevance to the intended career.
-   Suitability for Uganda and international learners.

Scores are optional. If used, define the scoring scale, provide a short
rationale and identify the evidence behind the assessment. Do not use
arbitrary scores to make listings look authoritative.

## 7. Job-market research and evidence

Course descriptions alone cannot establish employability. Research
should connect course skills to real work requirements.

### Suggested evidence sources

Use relevant, current sources such as:

-   Uganda job boards and employer career pages.
-   BrighterMonday Uganda: https://www.brightermonday.co.ug/
-   Fuzu: https://www.fuzu.com/
-   LinkedIn Jobs: https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/
-   Upwork: https://www.upwork.com/
-   Remote OK: https://remoteok.com/
-   Relevant international job boards and company vacancies.
-   Official provider curriculum, credential and pricing pages.

### Job-market evidence to capture

For each relevant sample vacancy: - Job title. - Employer. - Location or
remote status. - Date checked or published. - URL. - Skills and tools
requested. - Experience and qualification requirements. - Whether the
course addresses those requirements. - Gaps the learner would still need
to close.

Use multiple relevant postings where possible. A single vacancy is not
proof of broad market demand. Job evidence expires, so date it and
review it periodically.

### Evidence labels

Use these consistently throughout the catalogue:

-   **Verified:** Directly confirmed from an authoritative source.
-   **Supported:** Backed by multiple relevant sources or job postings.
-   **Editorial assessment:** The catalogue's reasoned interpretation or
    recommendation.
-   **Not verified:** Information that could not be confirmed; do not
    present it as fact.

Every material claim should have a source. Keep provider facts separate
from the catalogue's own judgement.

## 8. Free-versus-paid comparison component

Use a reusable comparison layout on each relevant course page.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Dimension               Free option             Paid option
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  Learning materials      Exact accessible        Additional content, if
                          content                 any

  Assessments             Included or restricted  Graded/assessed work

  Projects and labs       Available or limited    Included or expanded

  Certificate             Type, issuer or none    Type and issuer

  Verification            Available or not        Available or not

  Instructor support      Available or not        Available or not

  Cost                    UGX 0, if genuinely     Verified price and
                          free                    billing model

  Best suited to          Exploration and         Structured learning or
                          skill-building          credential needs
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Do not assume that paid always means better. Some strong providers offer
complete learning and useful credentials for free. Conversely, a paid
verified credential or professional exam may add value for a specific
career target.

## 10. Suggested technical implementation

### Recommended initial approach

Use Astro with structured content collections and Markdown/MDX records.
This fits a content-led catalogue and avoids introducing a database
before one is needed.

Suggested content structure:

``` text
src/
  content/
    config.ts
    courses/
      excel-skills.md
      google-data-analytics.md
      ibm-data-analyst.md
      cs50x.md
    pathways/
      data-analyst.md
      web-developer.md
      it-support.md
    categories/
      data-analytics.md
      programming.md
      digital-marketing.md

  components/
    CourseCard.astro
    CourseFilters.astro
    CourseOverview.astro
    SkillsList.astro
    CareerOpportunities.astro
    PricingComparison.astro
    LearningPathway.astro
    PortfolioProjects.astro
    SourceReferences.astro
```

Use Astro content collection schemas to validate required fields, types,
enums and references. Keep repeated presentation in reusable components.

### Data modelling guidance

Separate structured fields from long-form editorial content. Use
structured fields for: - Identity and categorisation. - Level and
duration. - Providers and official URLs. - Skills and tools. - Career
roles. - Learning options and cost models. - Credential details. -
Pathway relationships. - Region/access notes. - Source records. -
Verification dates and status.

Use Markdown body sections for detailed explanations, pros/cons,
recommendations and project descriptions.

Avoid storing the same course facts in multiple places. Reuse course
records in catalogue cards, related-course sections and pathway pages.

### Illustrative frontmatter

This is a starting example, not a complete final schema:

``` yaml
---
title: "Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate"
provider: "Google"
platform: "Coursera"
category: "Data Analytics"
level: "Beginner"

duration:
  estimate: "Variable"
  selfPaced: true

skills:
  - Excel
  - SQL
  - Data cleaning
  - Data visualisation

learningOptions:
  - type: "free"
    accessModel: "conditional"
    certificate: false
    url: "OFFICIAL_URL"

  - type: "paid"
    accessModel: "full-programme"
    certificate: true
    url: "OFFICIAL_URL"

careerRoles:
  - "Junior Data Analyst"
  - "Reporting Assistant"
  - "Business Intelligence Assistant"

regions:
  - "Uganda"
  - "International"

lastVerified: "2026-11-22"
researchStatus: "research-required"
---
```

Replace placeholder URLs and illustrative claims with verified
information before publication.

### When to introduce a database

Consider Supabase or another database only when there is a demonstrated
need for: - Multi-person editorial workflows. - An admin dashboard. -
Frequent updates by non-developers. - User accounts, saved courses or
progress. - Dynamic learner recommendations. - Larger-scale structured
job-market data.

Do not add a database solely because the catalogue has many entries.

## 11. Research workflow

Use the same research process for every course.

1.  Open the official provider course page.
2.  Verify that the course is active and enrolment is available.
3.  Capture the curriculum and learning outcomes.
4.  Verify prerequisites, level and duration.
5.  Test or confirm the free-access route.
6.  Verify paid access, price, billing and what payment adds.
7.  Identify the exact credential type and issuer.
8.  Check regional access and payment considerations for Uganda.
9.  Map the skills to plausible job roles.
10. Collect relevant employer/job-posting evidence.
11. Identify missing complementary skills.
12. Recommend next courses and progression.
13. Propose practical portfolio projects.
14. Write the editorial assessment.
15. Add source URLs, verification dates and uncertainty notes.
16. Complete editorial review before publication.

### Research status

Use a simple status field: - `research-required` - `in-research` -
`review-required` - `verified` - `published` - `needs-recheck` -
`archived`

A course should not be marked verified merely because its URL works. The
material facts must have been checked.

## 12. Editorial and quality standards

-   Write original, plain-language explanations.
-   Do not copy provider marketing text wholesale.
-   Link directly to official provider and enrolment pages.
-   Separate learning access from certificate or exam fees.
-   Distinguish completion certificates, digital badges, verified
    certificates, professional certifications, academic credit and
    accredited qualifications.
-   Do not describe an online awareness course as a professional
    licence.
-   Do not call a certificate "recognised by employers" without
    evidence.
-   Do not invent prices, durations, modules, prerequisites or
    job-market demand.
-   State when information varies by country or checkout.
-   Date prices and job-market evidence.
-   Use caveats where course content, access or credentials may change.
-   Make recommendations conditional on learner goals and existing
    ability.
-   Do not imply that a course guarantees employment, salary, promotion
    or freelance income.

A course is ready to publish only when the following are complete or
explicitly marked not applicable:

-   [ ] Official course page and enrolment link verified.
-   [ ] Provider and platform confirmed.
-   [ ] Course is active or its availability status is clearly stated.
-   [ ] Curriculum and learning outcomes reviewed.
-   [ ] Level, prerequisites and duration checked.
-   [ ] Intended learner and unsuitable learner cases described.
-   [ ] Skills and tools identified.
-   [ ] Free-access conditions verified.
-   [ ] Paid-access conditions and pricing verified or clearly marked
    unavailable.
-   [ ] Credential type and issuer accurately described.
-   [ ] Free-versus-paid recommendation written.
-   [ ] Relevant job roles identified without promising employment.
-   [ ] Uganda/international relevance assessed where evidence exists.
-   [ ] Job-market evidence linked and dated where used.
-   [ ] Complementary skills and gaps explained.

-   [ ] Practical project or portfolio suggestions included.
-   [ ] Editorial assessment justified.

-   [ ] Last-verified date recorded.
-   [ ] Editorial review completed.

## 16. Success measures

Measure whether the catalogue helps learners make better decisions, not
just whether it accumulates page views.

Possible measures: - Percentage of published courses with complete,
current research. - Percentage of course pages with verified official
links. - Search-to-course-detail engagement. - Click-throughs to
provider enrolment pages. - Use of free-course filters. - Career pathway
engagement. - Related-course exploration. - Learner feedback on clarity
and usefulness. - Broken-link and stale-information rate. - Number of
courses reviewed on schedule.

Do not treat provider click-throughs or course enrolments as proof of
employment outcomes.

## Final product direction

Build a trusted, Uganda-aware and internationally useful employability
learning catalogue. Its value should come from the quality of its
research, honest free-versus-paid guidance, realistic career mapping and
practical next steps.

The central editorial question for every course is:

**"After completing this course, what can the learner realistically do,
how can they demonstrate it, and what should they do next?"**

If every course page answers that question well, the catalogue will
offer substantially more value than a conventional list of online
courses.
