The campaign for his release has gained momentum after a series of controversial tweets from Gen. Muhoozi Kainerugaba’s X (formerly Twitter) account appeared to mock the situation.</p><blockquote data-media-max-width="560"><p dir="ltr" lang="en">Learning Runyankore. <a href="https://t.co/guh3FWAD1i">pic.twitter.com/guh3FWAD1i</a></p><p>— Muhoozi Kainerugaba (@mkainerugaba) <a href="https://twitter.com/mkainerugaba/status/1918027626972586322?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw">May 1, 2025</a></p></blockquote><p></p><p><strong><em>Edward Ssebufu </em></strong>also known as Eddie Mutwe, a well-known figure in Bobi Wine’s security detail, was reportedly seized by unidentified men in plain clothes. Friends and family say he vanished without a trace, sparking fears of yet another enforced disappearance—a pattern that has haunted government critics in Uganda for years.</p><p>Bobi Wine has been vocal about the incident, calling it an act of intimidation. —"<em><strong>They come for those close to me, thinking it will break me. One tweet read, —"<em><strong>Some people’s bodyguards think they are untouchable. Life can be funny.</strong></em>"</p><p>The posts were later deleted, but not before screenshots went viral, with many accusing Gen. Muhoozi of gloating over Eddie Mutwe’s disappearance.
