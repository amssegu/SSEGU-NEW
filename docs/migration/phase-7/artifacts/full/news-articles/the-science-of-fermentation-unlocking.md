Alcoholic fermentation is used in the production of beer, wine, and spirits, where yeasts transform sugars into ethanol and carbon dioxide. Lactic acid fermentation is utilized in making yogurt, kefir, and sauerkraut, involving various bacteria converting sugars into lactic acid. Acetic acid fermentation, involving Acetobacter bacteria, is used for making vinegar.</p></li><li><span>The Role of Microorganisms</span><p>Critical to the fermentation process are the microorganisms that drive these transformations. Yeasts, primarily Saccharomyaee cerevisiae, are often used in alcohol fermentation. For lactic acid fermentation, Lactobacillus species are prevalent. By converting perishable raw ingredients into more stable forms, fermentation helps inhibit the growth of pathogenic bacteria.
