On paper, the choice seems straightforward: compare the gigabytes, divide by the price and buy whichever network offers more.</p><p>In practice, Uganda's mobile data market is a little less tidy.</p><p><br></p><p>MTN Uganda and Airtel Uganda advertise bundles with different validity periods, access restrictions and purchasing conditions. Some are available through mobile apps, others are designed for online learning, and some offer generous allowances that expire within a narrow window. A bundle that looks remarkably cheap can become an expensive mistake if its data runs out before payday, or if the websites you need are not covered.</p><p><br></p><p>The real contest between MTN and Airtel is therefore not simply about who sells the most data. It is about which operator gives you the most usable internet for your money.</p><h2>MTN's small bundles appeal to people watching every shilling</h2><p>MTN's app-exclusive offers cater to customers who want to buy data in small amounts rather than commit a large sum at once. Its published options include 80MB for UGX 400, 280MB for UGX 700 and 450MB for UGX 1,500. Larger offers include 1.47GB for UGX 3,000 and 2.44GB for UGX 5,000.</p><p><br></p><p>For someone who mainly reads articles, checks email and exchanges WhatsApp messages, a small purchase can be a sensible way to control spending. There is little point buying several gigabytes if most of them will sit unused.</p><p>The larger offers become more interesting when a customer needs to download files, watch videos or spend a substantial part of the day online. At UGX 3,000 for 1.47GB, the advertised cost works out at approximately UGX 2,041 per gigabyte. The 2.44GB package at UGX 5,000 comes to about UGX 2,049 per gigabyte.</p><p><br></p><p>Those figures are competitive enough to attract attention, but there is a catch that deserves more attention than the price.</p><p>MTN's <a href="https://www.mtn.co.ug/helppersonal/app-exclusive-bundlesfaqs/">app-exclusive bundle FAQ</a> lists certain offers as valid for seven days. However, the operator's <a href="https://www.mtn.co.ug/app-exclusive-bundles-tcns/">published terms and conditions</a> state that the promotion's bundles expire at midnight on the day of purchase.</p><p><br></p><p>That is a material difference. Someone buying data on Monday expecting it to last until the following Monday could find themselves without the allowance they thought they had purchased.</p><p>Until MTN clarifies the discrepancy, customers should check the validity displayed in the MyMTN or MoMo app before paying and retain the confirmation message. The advertised allowance is only part of the deal; the expiry time determines how much of it is realistically useful.</p><p><br></p><h2>Airtel's bigger allowances come with their own conditions</h2><p>Airtel has offers that look more generous, particularly for customers who can plan their internet use around a particular time of the week.</p><p>Its published Freaky Weekend packages include 4.5GB for UGX 6,000, 5.2GB for UGX 7,000, 6GB for UGX 8,000 and 8GB for UGX 10,000. The packages also include voice minutes, and Airtel advertises their validity as running from Thursday at midnight until Monday at 6am.</p><p><br></p><p>The 8GB offer costs approximately UGX 1,250 per gigabyte, substantially less than the advertised cost of MTN's 2.44GB package. For someone planning to download films, catch up on videos or complete a week's worth of browsing over the weekend, that difference is worth considering.</p><p>But timing is everything. A person who needs internet on Tuesday cannot treat a weekend-only allowance as a substitute for a normal weekly bundle. Buying more data than you can use within its validity period is not a saving.</p><p><br></p><p>Airtel also advertises Work/Learn from Home bundles aimed at online education and meetings. The daily package costs UGX 1,500 for 1GB, while the weekly option costs UGX 9,000 for 7GB and the monthly package costs UGX 30,000 for 30GB.</p><p>These allowances are released at a rate of 1GB per day. Unused daily allocations expire at midnight rather than accumulating for a heavier-use day later in the week. The bundles are also restricted to selected educational and meeting services, including Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet and eligible educational websites.</p><p><br></p><p>For a student attending scheduled online lessons, that arrangement may be perfectly adequate. For a freelancer who needs to browse unrelated websites, download software or stream entertainment, the same offer is considerably less attractive.</p><p>The distinction matters because a gigabyte is not necessarily interchangeable with another gigabyte. The price may be lower, but the bundle is only useful if it covers the work you actually do.</p><p>The current offers and their conditions are listed on <a href="https://www.airtel.co.ug/ug/personal/data-deals-uganda">Airtel Uganda's data deals page</a>.</p><h2><br></h2><h2>What UGX 5,000, UGX 10,000 and a monthly budget can buy</h2><p>The best choice becomes clearer when the comparison starts with the money available rather than the network name.</p><p>With UGX 5,000, MTN's app-exclusive 2.44GB offer is worth checking, provided the validity is confirmed. For someone who needs general internet access, the important question is whether the data will remain available long enough to be used.</p><p><br></p><p>At UGX 10,000, Airtel's advertised 8GB Freaky Weekend package offers considerably more data per shilling, assuming the customer can use it within the weekend validity period. It is a stronger proposition for concentrated weekend use than for someone who needs a steady allowance throughout the week.</p><p>A monthly budget requires a different calculation altogether. A customer who spends UGX 1,500 every day could spend approximately UGX 45,000 over 30 days. That may be manageable for some users, but it is a substantial recurring expense for someone working with a tight budget.</p><p><br></p><p>MTN also publishes separate WakaNet bundles for MiFi devices. Its listed monthly option of 1.5GB for UGX 5,000 is not directly comparable with an ordinary phone bundle, because it is intended for a different product. Similarly, MTN's Freedom data offers are advertised as non-expiring, subject to their stated conditions and a 365-day usage period. Published examples include 1.3GB for UGX 5,000 and 4.5GB for UGX 15,000.</p><p>These longer-validity options can suit people who use data slowly, although their advertised price per gigabyte is higher than that of some promotional offers. Paying more per gigabyte can still be rational if it prevents unused data from expiring.</p><p>Details are available on the operators' official pages for <a href="https://www.mtn.co.ug/helppersonal/wakanet-mifi/">WakaNet</a> and <a href="https://www.mtn.co.ug/helppersonal/freedom-voice-and-data-bundles/">Freedom bundles</a>.</p><p><br></p><h2>The hidden cost of using mobile data</h2><p>Advertised prices tell only part of the story. The other part is how quickly a customer consumes the allowance.</p><p>Someone who mainly sends messages and reads text-heavy websites can stretch a small bundle considerably further than someone who watches high-resolution video. Video calls, automatic app updates, cloud backups and laptop hotspot use can also consume data faster than expected.</p><p><br></p><p>A laptop is particularly easy to underestimate. Connecting a phone to a computer may trigger software updates or cloud synchronisation that would otherwise happen over Wi-Fi. A bundle that seems generous on a phone can disappear surprisingly quickly when shared with another device.</p><p>The simplest way to understand your own requirements is to examine mobile data usage in your phone's settings. The breakdown by application often reveals whether video, social media or background activity is responsible for most of the spending.</p><p><br></p><p>This is also where network reliability enters the calculation. A cheap bundle is of little use if the connection repeatedly drops during an online meeting or becomes painfully slow at the time you need it.</p><p>MTN may perform better at one address, while Airtel may be more reliable elsewhere. Indoor reception, congestion, device compatibility and the available network technology all influence the experience. National coverage claims cannot establish which SIM will work best inside a particular home or workplace.</p><p><br></p><p>If possible, test both networks where you regularly use the internet, especially during busy evening hours. The <a href="https://www.ucc.co.ug/">Uganda Communications Commission</a> is a useful starting point for broader information about the country's telecommunications sector, but local testing remains the more practical guide to choosing between the two.</p><h2><br></h2><h2>So, which network offers better value?</h2><p>For a customer who wants small, affordable purchases, MTN's app-exclusive offers are worth examining, although the validity discrepancy needs to be resolved before the weekly-labelled bundles can be recommended without qualification.</p><p>For someone who can make full use of a weekend allowance, Airtel's published Freaky Weekend packages offer more advertised data per shilling than the MTN app-exclusive examples considered here. Its Work/Learn bundles can also suit students and remote workers, provided their daily limits and website restrictions match their needs.</p><p><br></p><p>Neither conclusion makes one operator universally cheaper. The comparison changes with the budget, validity period, permitted websites and signal quality at the point of use.</p><p>Before buying, check the current offer in the operator's official app or USSD menu. Promotions can change, and a published webpage may not reflect every offer available to an individual customer.</p><p><br></p><p>The most useful measure is not the number of gigabytes printed beside the price.
