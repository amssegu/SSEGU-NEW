<p>Before humans ever walked on the Moon or dreamed of reaching Mars, two unlikely heroes helped make space travel possible—chimpanzees named <strong>Ham</strong> and <strong>Enos</strong>. Their journeys into space weren’t just experiments; they were groundbreaking missions that proved life could survive beyond Earth.</p>

<p>But what really happened to these “Astrochimps”? And more importantly, what is the status of chimpanzees today? Let’s dive into this fascinating story that blends space history with wildlife conservation.</p>

<h2>The Unsung Heroes of Space: Ham &amp; Enos 🚀🐒</h2>

<p>In the early 1960s, the race to space was heating up. Scientists needed to understand how living beings would react to spaceflight before sending humans. That’s where Ham and Enos came in.</p>

<h3>Ham: The First Chimp in Space</h3>

<p>On January 31, 1961, Ham made history by becoming the first great ape to travel into space. His mission was suborbital, meaning he traveled to space and returned without orbiting the Earth.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Successfully completed tasks during flight</li>
  <li>Proved that astronauts could function in space</li>
  <li>Survived the intense launch and re-entry</li>
</ul>

<p>After his mission, Ham lived a relatively peaceful life in zoos. He passed away in 1983, leaving behind a legacy that helped pave the way for human spaceflight.</p>
