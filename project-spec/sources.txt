## 1. How to read this document

- **Start with** means a sensible first source to implement, subject to the listed checks.
- **Link/metadata only** means import a small amount of factual listing information and send visitors to the original provider. Do not copy course materials or full descriptions.
- **Approval required** means do not activate automated ingestion until the source's access requirements are satisfied.
- **Do not use for JobPosting markup** means do not add Google's `JobPosting` structured data to imported listings from that source, even if the page itself is indexed normally.

The website may eventually earn advertising revenue. That makes non-commercial licences especially important: a source's permission to use material for non-commercial education does not automatically permit reuse on an ad-supported commercial website.

# Part B — Course and learning sources

## 7. Course-source policy

For the first version, treat course providers as **catalogue/link sources**, not as content libraries to copy.

Store factual discovery metadata such as:
- Course title.
- Provider.
- Subject/category.
- Level, if stated.
- Delivery mode, if stated.
- Duration, if stated.
- Whether study is free, paid or free-to-audit, only when verified.
- Whether a certificate is free, paid, unavailable or unknown.
- Original course URL.
- Date last checked.

Do not copy course videos, notes, readings, assignments, images or full descriptions unless the specific licence permits it and the website's commercial status is compatible with that licence.

## 8. MIT OpenCourseWare (MIT OCW)

**Role:** University-level learning materials and self-study courses.  
**Priority:** Link to course pages; do not republish course materials on an ad-supported site without permission.

- Main site: https://ocw.mit.edu/
- Terms: https://www.ocw.mit.edu/pages/privacy-and-terms-of-use/
- Reuse guidance: https://mitocw.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/5093047407771-Reusing-MIT-OpenCourseWare-Content
- Course overview: https://ocw.mit.edu/pages/get-started/

### Important licence point

MIT OCW's materials are generally under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 unless a page/item says otherwise. The NonCommercial restriction means those materials cannot be used commercially. Some third-party material is marked “All rights reserved” and is not covered by the general OCW licence. MIT OCW does not provide credit or certificates for completing its materials.

### What must be done

1. Use MIT OCW as a link-out course catalogue source initially.
2. Identify course pages and collect only basic factual metadata needed to help users discover them.
3. Link each record to the exact MIT course page.
4. Label the offering accurately as self-study/open course material; do not imply it awards a certificate or academic credit.
5. Do not download, mirror or republish notes, videos, assignments, images or course text on this website.
6. Do not reuse CC BY-NC-SA course materials in an ad-supported/commercial context without permission.
7. If future plans require copying any materials, review the licence on every item and obtain permission where necessary.
8. Store a source-specific note that content reuse is restricted by the non-commercial licence.

**Initial status:** Link-only catalogue candidate. Do not republish course content under the assumption that “free to access” means commercially reusable.

## 9. OpenLearn — The Open University

**Role:** Free courses and learning resources.  
**Priority:** Link-out catalogue only unless the provider confirms commercial syndication rights.

- OpenLearn: https://www.open.edu/openlearn/
- FAQ/licensing: https://www.open.edu/openlearn/about-openlearn/frequently-asked-questions-on-openlearn
- Copyright policy: https://about.open.ac.uk/strategy-and-policies/policies-and-statements/copyright-openlearn

### Important licence point

OpenLearn says much of its own course content is CC BY-NC-SA, with third-party materials and exceptions identified on individual pages. Its FAQ explains that aggregation of its content is not considered adaptation for the purpose of the non-commercial restriction. It also says that linking to OpenLearn pages is welcome, provided the site does not imply affiliation or endorsement.

### What must be done

1. Start by linking to OpenLearn course pages rather than copying their content.
2. Collect only factual catalogue metadata necessary for discovery.
3. Link directly to the provider's course page.
4. Do not imply that this website is affiliated with or endorsed by The Open University.
5. Do not copy course text, videos, images or other learning materials into the site.
6. Check each course's acknowledgements and special restrictions before any reuse.
7. Because the website may display advertising, ask OpenLearn for written clarification before using any syndicated content or metadata feed in a commercial context.
8. Record the applicable licence/terms URL and review date.

**Initial status:** Link-only candidate. Commercial reuse of content needs clarification; do not treat aggregation as adaptation.

## 10. Saylor Academy

**Role:** Self-paced courses across a range of subjects.

- Licensing information: https://www.saylor.org/LicensingInformation

### What must be done

6. Distinguish free course access from any paid certificate or assessment fee.
7. Do not copy third-party resources embedded in Saylor courses unless their individual licences permit it.
8. Record the licence for each course/resource if any content is ever reused.

**Initial status:** Link-only until API access and metadata terms are confirmed.

## 11. OER Commons

### Important licence point

**Initial status:** Link-only candidate; manual curation is acceptable for the first release.

# Part C — What to build

## 13. Shared source-ingestion work

Build the ingestion system once, then add one adapter per source.

### Source registry

For every source, store:

- `name`
- `source_type` (`job` or `course`)
- `base_url`
- `feed_url` or API endpoint
- `permission_type`
- `terms_url`
- `attribution_text`
- `enabled`
- `last_attempt_at`
- `last_success_at`
- `notes` for source-specific restrictions

Suggested permission values:

- `metadata_only`
- `redistribute_with_attribution`
- `open_license`
- `link_only`
- `permission_required`

Add source-specific flags where necessary, for example:
- `requires_appname`
- `requires_api_key`
- `must_use_source_application_link`
- `prohibit_jobposting_schema`
- `remove_when_expired`
- `commercial_reuse_allowed`
- `manual_review_required`

Do not rely on a single generic permission flag for restrictions such as Himalayas' prohibition on third-party syndication and JobPosting structured data.

## 14. Job importer tasks

- [ ] Create a common job record shape.
- [ ] Implement one adapter per approved job source.
- [ ] Store the source's stable ID and original URL.
- [ ] Preserve source-specific application URL rules.
- [ ] Normalise employer, location, employment type, work mode and dates.
- [ ] Keep salary currency and salary period explicit.
- [ ] Deduplicate across sources without merging unrelated vacancies.
- [ ] Preserve source attribution on every detail page.
- [ ] Store source-specific SEO restrictions.
- [ ] Expire jobs only with reliable evidence.
- [ ] Remove expired jobs from active lists and active sitemap output.
- [ ] Ensure a failed source fetch never expires all of its existing records.
- [ ] Run jobs no more than twice daily.

## 15. Course importer tasks

- [ ] Start with link-only course records.
- [ ] Create a common course record shape.
- [ ] Store exact provider/course URL.
- [ ] Store title, provider, subject, level and duration only when verified.
- [ ] Separate `course_price_status` from `certificate_price_status`.
- [ ] Store the licence/terms URL and content reuse classification.
- [ ] Do not store course files, videos or lesson text in the first version.
- [ ] Add an availability check only if permitted and reliable.
- [ ] Mark stale records as `unverified`; do not silently claim a course is still available.
- [ ] Run course refreshes no more than twice weekly.

## 16. Build and deployment tasks

1. Use GitHub Actions for the existing schedule:
   - Jobs: 06:15 and 18:15 EAT (03:15 and 15:15 UTC).
   - Courses: Tuesday and Friday at 07:00 EAT (04:00 UTC).
2. Fetch only sources enabled in the source registry.
3. Validate, normalise and deduplicate.
4. Update Cloudflare D1.
5. Export approved records to JSON before the Astro build.
6. Generate static detail pages with Astro `getStaticPaths()`.
7. Build the sitemap from eligible canonical pages.
8. Deploy only when public data changed.
9. Keep ordinary visitor requests static; do not query D1 or invoke a Worker for every page view.

## 17. Recommended implementation order

| Order | Source | Why |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | JubaJobs jobs feed | Regional relevance and explicit partner-feed pathway |
| 2 | YubHub selected country/graduate feeds | Broad feed formats and stated attribution-based redistribution |
| 3 | Remote OK | Adds remote-work coverage with a simple public feed |
| 4 | ReliefWeb | Valuable NGO/development vacancies, but requires approved appname |
| 5 | Himalayas | Useful remote listings, but source-specific Apply, attribution, expiry and SEO restrictions |
| 6 | Saylor Academy | Course catalogue link-out; investigate API access |
| 7 | MIT OCW | Link-out catalogue; non-commercial restriction on course materials |
| 8 | OpenLearn | Link-out catalogue; non-commercial and per-resource restrictions |
| 9 | freeCodeCamp | Curated link-out learning paths |
| 10 | OER Commons | Add only after confirming a permitted metadata access method |

This order is a practical build sequence, not a legal determination that every source is already approved.

## 18. Launch checklist

### Before a source is enabled

- [ ] Its official feed/API is confirmed working.
- [ ] Its current terms have been read and saved.
- [ ] Commercial/ad-supported use has been checked.
- [ ] Attribution and original links are implemented.
- [ ] Rate limits and required credentials are understood.
- [ ] The adapter passes a sample-data test.
- [ ] Duplicate handling is tested.
- [ ] Source outage behaviour is tested.
- [ ] Expiry/unavailability behaviour is tested.
- [ ] Source-specific SEO restrictions are enforced.
- [ ] The source is recorded in D1 with `enabled = true` only after approval.

### First release recommendation

Start with **JubaJobs + selected YubHub feeds** for regional job coverage. Add **Remote OK** for remote roles. Work on **ReliefWeb** after receiving its appname approval. Add **Himalayas** only after implementing its stricter application-link, attribution, expiry and no-JobPosting rules.

For courses, begin with a small **link-only catalogue** rather than a scraper. Use Saylor, MIT OCW, OpenLearn and freeCodeCamp as provider destinations, and investigate OER Commons access separately. This avoids turning “free to learn” into an unsupported assumption that the site's commercial project can republish the course content.
